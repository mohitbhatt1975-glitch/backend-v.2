# Ladakh Shelter Thermal Simulator — v5

Predicts indoor temperature, solar gain and heat flow for high-altitude shelters
in Ladakh, against live NASA POWER weather. Built for Smart India Hackathon.

**Run it:** `pip install -r requirements.txt`, then `uvicorn main:app`, then open
<http://127.0.0.1:8000/>. The dashboard and the API are one service.

---

## What the problem statement asks for, and where it is

| PS requirement | Where |
|---|---|
| Prediction of shelter inside temperature | `Inside_Temp` — temperature chart |
| Prediction of thermal energy from solar radiation | `Solar_Gain` — heat-flow chart |
| Heat flow details over a time period | `Conduction_Loss` **+** `Infiltration_Loss`, hourly ×24 |
| Effect of openings | `Window_U_Value` (glazing conduction) and `ACH` (infiltration) |
| Composite multi-material walls | `materials_library.py` → `POST /materials/composite` |
| Orientation | `Orientation_Factor`, scales solar gain |
| Comparative analysis of materials | `POST /compare`, ranked by comfort degree-hours |
| Real-time data | NASA POWER, fetched per request and cached |

Heat flow is split by *mechanism* rather than reported as one lumped number, so
"where is the heat going?" has an answer: conduction escapes **through** the
fabric (fix with insulation), infiltration is carried **around** it by leaking
air (fix by sealing).

## Architecture

```
physics.py            the energy balance — single source of truth
solar.py              sun position, and irradiance on window / wall / roof planes
  ├── generate_data.py   builds 48,000 rows of training data from it
  │     └── train_model.py  trains the XGBoost surrogate, reports held-out error
  └── main.py            answers /simulate from it exactly, and scores the
                         surrogate against it on every request
static/index.html     dashboard (served at /)
```

### Why the API serves physics, not the ML model

v2 trained XGBoost on data generated by a formula, then served *the model's
approximation of that formula*. That is strictly worse than the formula: slower,
and wrong in ways that are physically impossible. It produced negative solar gain
at midnight, conduction changing sign at dawn, and indoor air colder than ambient
inside an occupied shelter.

v3 evaluates `physics.py` directly, so those outputs cannot occur. The surrogate
is still trained and still reported — `/simulate` returns `surrogate_agreement`,
scoring XGBoost against the engine on that exact request. That is a real ML
result ("the surrogate reproduces the engine to N °C RMSE") rather than a
liability, and it is the honest version of the ML claim.

### Integration

The balance is a linear first-order ODE over each hour, integrated in closed
form:

```
T_ss = T_out + Q_in/UA          T(t) = T_ss + (T_0 − T_ss)·e^(−t/τ),  τ = C/UA
```

v2 used forward Euler over a full hour with a ±6 °C/hr clamp. A canvas tent has
τ ≈ 4 minutes, so hour-long Euler steps overshoot badly — the clamp was hiding
instability, not preventing it. The exponential form cannot overshoot, so indoor
air can never fall below ambient while the shelter is heated. Verified over
20,000 random configurations: zero direction violations, energy balance closing
to 2.4e-12 relative error.

## Metrics, and how to read them

**Comfort degree-hours** — how far, and for how long, indoor temperature sits
outside 15–25 °C. v2 deducted a flat penalty per hour outside the band, so every
Ladakh January shelter scored identically (nothing clears 15 °C in winter). The
0–100 score is derived from degree-hours and never saturates.

**Heating load / kerosene saved** — energy to *hold* 15 °C for 24 h, versus an
uninsulated leaky shelter of the same size. v2 summed the imbalance at whatever
temperature the shelter drifted to, which is zero at steady state by
construction. Read the basis honestly: holding 15 °C in a canvas tent in January
is deliberately punishing, and nobody actually burns that much fuel — they wear
layers and accept a colder room. It is a like-for-like thermal comparison, not a
claim about current consumption. Absolute loads are returned alongside the saving
so the number can be checked.

Representative output, Leh, 15 Jan 2026 (outdoor −18.1 to −4.3 °C, site 4533 m,
ground −2.8 °C):

| Build | Indoor | Score | Degree-hours | Heating load |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canvas tent | −19.7 to 4.2 °C | 14 | 599 | 399 kWh/d |
| Mud brick | 3.6 to 9.6 °C | 49 | 211 | 111 kWh/d |
| Insulated panel | 3.4 to 14.4 °C | 62 | 145 | 15 kWh/d |

Where the heat goes, insulated panel over 24 h: walls 39%, roof 22%, draughts
20%, windows 10%, floor 9%. That split is the actionable output — insulation
fixes the first two, sealing the third, glazing the fourth.

Held-out surrogate accuracy (20% test split):

| Target | RMSE | R² |
|---|---|---|
| Inside_Temp | 0.943 °C | 0.9911 |
| Solar_Gain | 26.0 W | 0.9981 |
| Conduction_Loss | 1395.9 W | 0.8333 |
| Infiltration_Loss | 124.8 W | 0.9531 |

Conduction is a harder target in v5 than v4 (R² 0.887 → 0.833) because roof and
wall now sit at different sol-air temperatures. The physics is more correct; the
surrogate simply has more to learn.

## What v4 and v5 corrected

**v4 — altitude.** Air at Leh's 4533 m is 57% of sea-level pressure, but the
engine used sea-level constants for indoor air heat capacity and infiltration,
overstating infiltration loss by 1.8×. `physics.air_density_ratio()` now scales
both from the elevation NASA returns with the weather.

**v4 — solar plane.** `ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN` is global *horizontal* irradiance;
windows are vertical. At 34° N in January a south-facing wall catches **1.48×**
what the ground does, and in June only 0.42× — the correct seasonal reversal.
`solar.py` splits the measurement into beam and diffuse (Erbs) and projects it
onto each plane.

**v5 — the floor meets the ground, not the air.** Undisturbed soil sits near the
annual mean temperature (−2.8 °C here, from NASA's climatology) rather than the
−18 °C night air, with the soil's own resistance in series. Treating the floor
as one more cold wall overstated losses; it is now about 5% of the total.

**v5 — sunlit surfaces and the night sky.** Opaque walls and roofs absorb
sunlight and radiate to a cold sky. Both are folded into **sol-air temperature**,
the standard device: `T_sol-air = T_out + αI/h_o − ΔR/h_o`. A roof under a clear
night sky behaves as if the air were 3.9 °C colder; in full sun a wall behaves
as if it were ~24 °C warmer.

The effect is largest on light, thin structures. A canvas tent now runs −19.7 to
+4.2 °C over a January day, where v4 gave −17.5 to −3.3: warmed by sun on the
fabric, and dipping *below* air temperature at night through radiation — which is
real, and the same reason frost forms on clear nights when the air is above zero.

## Known limitations

The engine is now internally exact — the energy balance closes to 1e-12 — but
**exact is not the same as accurate**. Every accuracy figure above compares the
surrogate to the engine, never the engine to a real shelter. Nothing here has
been validated against measurement, and that is the single biggest gap.

Physics still simplified or absent:

- **Absorptivity is one fixed value (0.6)** for the whole envelope. A
  whitewashed wall (0.3) and dark stone (0.9) behave very differently, and that
  is a cheap, real design lever this does not yet expose.
- **Ground temperature is the annual mean**, with no seasonal swing or depth
  profile.
- **Single lumped thermal mass** — no temperature gradient or time lag through
  wall thickness, which is the very mechanism thermal mass works by. Thick mud
  brick is where this costs most.
- **Infiltration is a fixed ACH**, unresponsive to wind or stack effect, even
  though wind does scale conduction.
- **Wind is a linear fudge** (`1 + 0.05v`), not a surface film-coefficient model.
- **No moisture, latent heat or stratification**; single zone throughout.
- **Comfort band 15–25 °C is a generic office band.** Acclimatised occupants
  have a materially lower neutral temperature; adaptive comfort would move every
  score.
- **NASA POWER's hourly solar lags its meteorology by months.** Where no recent
  day is complete the API falls back to the same calendar window in a previous
  year and flags it as `weather_is_seasonal_fallback`.

**So state the claim carefully:** this is a design-*comparison* tool, not a
calibrated predictor. The errors above push every build the same direction and
largely cancel in a comparison, so "this build needs a seventh of the fuel of
that one" is supportable. "Your shelter will be 4.1 °C at dawn" is not.

## Endpoints

`GET /` dashboard · `POST /simulate` · `POST /compare` · `GET /materials` ·
`POST /materials/composite` · `GET /health` · `GET /docs`

Inputs are bounded by Pydantic and validated jointly — glazing may not exceed
60% of the envelope, so a 3 m dome cannot carry 15 m² of glass.

## Deploy

`Dockerfile` works unchanged on Render, Hugging Face Spaces and Fly.io. The
service is ~185 MB resident, inside a 512 MB free tier. `render.yaml` is a
blueprint for Render; `training_data.csv` is excluded from the image since it is
only needed to retrain.

Render's free tier sleeps after 15 minutes idle (~50 s cold start). A free
uptime pinger against `/health` every 10 minutes keeps it warm.
